If this sounds familiar

Good advice from someone who doesn't know your business model.

Most business advice arrives at an MSP slightly out of focus. The consultant is capable and the thinking is sound, but they have never carried a contract base, never watched utilisation quietly decide whether the month worked, and never had to explain to a client why the project they signed in March still isn't finished. You end up funding their education in your business before you get anything back.

The alternative is usually a peer or a vendor coach who knows the industry cold and has never actually run the company. They can tell you what your service board should look like. They cannot help you with a leadership team that nods and then does what it likes, or a partner conversation you have been avoiding for a year.

What is genuinely rare is someone who has done both jobs. That is the gap I sit in, and it is the reason MSP owners tend to get further with me in the first month than the work would normally allow.

Why me

Nearly two decades in managed services, a decade of it as the owner.

I have worked in managed services since 2008, first running operations for someone else's provider and then, for a decade, owning and running my own. I grew it to around thirty people, through rapid growth, a pandemic that took most of the revenue out from under us almost overnight, serious financial trouble, and a recovery that ended in a successful exit. Before managed services there were a dozen years in IT, hands on, which is where the technical grounding comes from.

Which means I have sat in your chair for the parts nobody puts in a case study. Making payroll when the bank balance said no. Letting good people go in a single afternoon. Running ConnectWise PSA for the best part of twenty years and living with every configuration decision we made early and regretted later. Discovering that the client I could least afford to lose was the one I had built the least protection around.

What I bring to your business is not a framework with my name on it. It is judgment earned expensively in a company that looked a lot like yours, pointed at your situation, and honest about the parts I do not know.

Common questions

Working with service providers, answered plainly.

Do you take on work with MSPs and IT service providers specifically?

Yes. The work is the same work I do with any owner-operated business, but I owned and ran an MSP myself, so you do not have to spend the first two months explaining recurring revenue, ticket economics or why a good month on the P&L can still be a bad month for cash.

Is this technical consulting?

No. I am not coming in to design your stack, tune your RMM or tell your engineers how to do their jobs. The work is the business around the technology: direction, structure, the numbers, the leadership team and the operating rhythm. If you want that separated out, the operations work is the closest fit.

My business is not an MSP, but it is technology adjacent. Does that still fit?

Usually. The pattern that matters is a service business with contracted revenue, a delivery team and an owner still holding too much of it together. That describes a lot of technology service providers, not only managed services.

Will you be working with my competitors?

Not in the same market. I keep engagements clear of each other geographically and by segment, and I will tell you before we start if there is any overlap worth knowing about.

What does the first conversation look like?

A straight talk about what is actually going on in the business and whether I can help. Peer to peer rather than a sales call, and short. If it is not a fit I will say so.
